## Signal and SNR conventions

Accelerations are SI: `1 microGal = 1e-8 m/s^2`, `1 nGal = 1e-11 m/s^2`, and
`1 ng = 9.80665e-9 m/s^2`. All noise ASDs are **one-sided**.

The exact Newtonian waveform is sampled over one mechanical turn.
`harmonic_coefficient` returns `2 mean(a(phi) exp(-i n phi))`; its magnitude
is the peak amplitude of harmonic n. Link budgets use n=2 by default. Raw
waveform peak and total RMS remain separate diagnostics. Equal opposite masses
suppress odd harmonics; balance alone does not. The radial asymptotic
quadrupole amplitude is `9*G*Q/(4*d^4)` for the stated in-plane geometry.

For one selected harmonic with peak amplitude A in a channel of bandwidth B:

```
signal mean square = A^2/2
noise mean square  = ASD^2 * B
power SNR          = A^2/(2 * ASD^2 * B)
Gaussian benchmark = B * log2(1 + SNR)
```

This Gaussian benchmark does not establish a mechanically achievable rate.
The multicarrier helper uses the same squared-ASD convention and assumes
independent carriers in nonoverlapping bands.

Observation time is distinct from channel bandwidth. A known-frequency tone
observed over an integer number of cycles has coherent estimator
`SNR = A^2*T/ASD^2` (equivalent ENBW `1/(2T)` under the formula above). The
magnetic comparison uses this **power** SNR with T=1/f. Bench outputs keep 1/B
as a reciprocal-bandwidth diagnostic and separately report coherent T.

## Receiver assumptions

`RECEIVERS` contains two explicitly qualified flat-noise references:

1. **MEMS floor extrapolation:** 0.1 microGal/sqrt(Hz), measured by Gao at
   0.14 Hz, not at the modeled carriers; reported bandwidth is 108 Hz.
2. **Carter broadband reference:** 0.1 ng/sqrt(Hz) = 98.0665 nGal/sqrt(Hz).
   This scalar is not a measured flat spectrum across arbitrary signal bands.

The constant `opto_res=0.1 nGal/sqrt(Hz)` tier is removed. Flat-reference
capacity curves retain B=fc/2 for diagnostic comparison with the earlier draft
and are labeled as assumptions, not device-supported performance.

**Resonant operation is modeled separately** by `StructuralOscillator`:

- Carter's reported m=3.1 g, f0=50.3 Hz and Q=637000.
- Assumed 300 K and idealized flat displacement readout 100 fm/sqrt(Hz).
  These do not constitute a fit to the measured noise spectrum.
- Structural-damping thermal ASD `sqrt(8*pi*k*T*f0^2/(m*f*Q))`.
- Displacement/acceleration susceptibility
  `1/((2*pi)^2 * hypot(f0^2-f^2, f0^2/Q))`, in s^2.
- Independent thermal and input-referred readout PSDs add.

The modeled thermal floor at resonance is about 5.149 nGal/sqrt(Hz). Readout
noise improves near resonance; thermal noise is not divided by Q a second
time. No hard communication cutoff at f0/Q is imposed.

The resonant table evaluates B=1, 0.01 and 0.0001 Hz. It uses the sum of
maximum thermal PSD and maximum readout PSD over `[fc-B/2, fc+B/2]` as a
conservative constant bound on the idealized device's noise throughout the
band. It is not exact colored-noise capacity. At SNR=10 the resulting
white-noise benchmarks are 3.459, 0.03459 and 0.0003459 bit/s, not measured or
actuator-constrained rates. The 0.2-200 Hz model domain excludes the reported
209 Hz higher mode; it is not a claim of validated hardware bandwidth.

The reference table tunes the rotor toward a fixed 50.3 Hz receiver,
leaving B/2 received-frequency headroom below its assumed tip-speed ceiling.
The Fixed class is off resonance in that **reference-device comparison**.
This is not a fundamental restriction: f0, temperature, mass, Q and readout
are configurable `StructuralOscillator` parameters.

`receiver_design_sweep.csv` separately evaluates the reference design at
300 K and **custom frequency-matched receivers** at 300 K, 77 K and 4 K.
For the Fixed class, `tune_receiver=True` lowers f0 to its selected carrier,
so it too operates at resonance. This sweep holds m, Q and optical readout
fixed as explicit design assumptions, not measured redesigned-sensor
specifications. Changing flexures or temperature can change Q, stiffness,
readout and higher modes; those changes require design-specific evidence.
At resonance the assumed thermal floor scales as sqrt(T*f0/(m*Q)); readout
noise is not automatically reduced by cooling. Fig. 10 displays both the
resonant thermal scaling and the readout limitation over wider signal bands.

Installed support motion, Newtonian noise, detailed control noise and rotor
contamination remain to be specified. Peterson markers represent 10 Hz ground
motion, not measured gravitational noise at the carrier.

## Mechanics and remaining limits

Deployment tip speeds and power entries are assumptions, not validated
hardware designs. Energy, inertia and tie loads refer to point masses,
excluding arm inertia/self-stress and rotor dynamics. `energy_loss_power`
converts an explicit fractional loss rate to power: an illustrative 1%/h
implies 4.34 W Bench, 4.34 kW Fixed and 278 kW Heavy. The CSV distinguishes
this example from legacy total-power assumptions.

`modulation_step` computes torque, initial/peak mechanical power and signed
energy change for a finite linear frequency ramp. The +1 Hz diagnostic step
starts at the legacy maximum-speed carrier; the output explicitly flags that
it exceeds the assumed speed ceiling. It illustrates actuator demand rather
than an achievable operating point. A configurable constrained actuator is
now available separately, as described below. Regenerative efficiency,
an encoder/decoder and BER, finite-mass geometry and a validated vacuum-loss
model remain outside the model.

## Constrained actuator

`gravcomm.actuator.Actuator` requires explicit total driven inertia, peak and
continuous torque, torque-response time, motoring and regenerative mechanical
power limits, and maximum mechanical speed. Optional viscous drag is specified
in N m per rad/s. Include arm, carrier and motor inertia in the input as needed.
There are no inferred motor ratings or claims that these ratings are available
in a particular machine.

`simulate_actuator` takes time samples and feedforward torque samples, initial
speed, optional initial phase/demand, and optional target carrier and proportional
speed gain. Inputs are linearly interpolated. Its equations are

```
command = feedforward + Kp * (target_omega - omega)
response_time * demand_dot = command - demand
actual_torque = clip(demand, speed-dependent torque/power envelope)
inertia * omega_dot = actual_torque - viscous_drag * omega
phase_dot = omega
```

At zero response time the demand follows the command instantaneously. The
envelope permits constant torque at low speed and constant power at high speed,
with separate motoring and absorption limits and both rotation directions.
It approximates drive capability without an explicit voltage/current motor model.
Filtering precedes clipping; it does not guarantee a bound on actual torque slew
when the envelope changes. There is no controller integrator or anti-windup state.
For a nonzero response time, the initial demand defaults to zero; initialize it
to the steady drag-compensation torque when modeling an already-running rotor.

The result contains actual speed, phase, filtered demand, torque, signed power,
motoring/absorbed work, drag heat, and the work-energy residual. Peak diagnostics
include internal RK4 evaluations. Continuous torque is checked against RMS over
the simulated interval, a repeated-duty proxy rather than a thermal transient
model. Speed and RMS violations are reported without clamping speed. Mechanical
absorption is not a prediction of electricity returned to the supply.

`trace.acceleration(rotor, distance)` uses the achieved phase and the complex
second-harmonic spatial coefficient, preserving its sign/phase. Pass
`harmonic=None` for the full Newtonian field including its static component.
This output is ready for subsequent receiver/decoder work; no receiver response,
noise or BER is implicitly simulated. Resolve the carrier in the output sampling
when processing this waveform, and refine `max_step_s` to check convergence near
limiter transitions.

Run the standalone demonstration from the project root:

```powershell
py AIP_Advances_submission/scripts/simulate_actuator.py
```

Outputs go to `results/corrected_model/actuator/`: four time-history CSVs,
`summary.csv`, all scenario parameters in `assumptions.json`, and an actuator
comparison PNG/PDF. The example uses a 35 Hz carrier with a smooth 0.4 Hz
excursion, inertia 200000 kg m^2, assumed 250 kN m peak/150 kN m continuous
torque, 25 MW motoring/20 MW absorption, and 125 rad/s speed ceiling.
It compares instantaneous demand, 50 ms and 200 ms filters, and 50 ms with
speed feedback. These are illustrative assumptions, not an optimized design.
The feedback case exceeds its assumed continuous-torque rating and is flagged;
filtering alone does not eliminate tracking errors or establish a bit rate.

## Sources and validation

A numerical seven-tone CF-FSK desktop operating point is now specified in
`sections/desktop_cf_fsk.tex` and included in the manuscript. The selected
rounded 30 mm spoke design supports a conditional modeled 0.333 bit/s at
0.5 m from the axis, with 8 s symbols and 1.091 N m peak inertial torque.
See `results/desktop_cf_fsk/README.md` for actuator/receiver assumptions,
conditional symbol-error trials, technical-noise sensitivity and reproduction.
This is a numerical design target, not a hardware-link or safe-speed rating.

The shaped demonstrator now has a separate finite-volume source model,
`FiniteRotor`, whose quadrature NPZ files, phase traces and harmonic spectra
are generated by `fea/shaped_waveforms.py`. It includes carrier and finite steel
inserts. See `fea/STRESS_MODES_AND_DESIGN.md` for matched stresses, identified
modes, the user's fixed-mass objective and explicit junction-fillet studies.
The previous blanket statement that a spoked carrier contributes no AC gravity
has been removed from the mechanics documentation.

An initial Gmsh/CalculiX study is available in `fea/`, using Ubuntu WSL.
It follows the shaped spoked carrier in `../cad/bench_source_disk.scad`:
two approximately 2 kg inserts, 250 mm carrier radius, and 1800 rpm maximum.
This is distinct from the 50 kg point-mass Bench deployment used in the
link-budget tables. See `fea/README.md` for geometry and boundary-condition
assumptions, and `fea/INITIAL_RESULTS.md` for benchmark and convergence results.
The first model has a fixed bore and ideal bonded inserts; it does not yet
validate attachment hardware, bearings or shaft critical speeds.

- Carter et al., [Scientific Reports 14, 17775 (2024)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-68623-0), Eq. (3), oscillator parameters, Figs. 3 and 5.
- Gao et al., [Microsystems & Nanoengineering 12, 8 (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41378-025-01039-6), noise at 0.14 Hz and feedback bandwidth.

Tests include independent Gauss-Legendre field integration, Parseval's
identity, harmonic symmetry and sampling convergence, radial/transverse
quadrupole limits, differential phasors, fluctuation-dissipation at resonance,
finite-band noise bounds, estimator normalization, low-SNR capacity, unit
conversion and the work-energy identity for modulation.
